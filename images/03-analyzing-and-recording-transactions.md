# Figure briefs — Chapter 3: Analyzing and Recording Transactions

## Figure 3.1 (optional): The accounting equation visualized

**Placement:** §3.3, after the equation is introduced.

**Description:** A balance-scale diagram with Assets on one side, Liabilities + Equity on the other. Three sample transactions illustrated below the scale showing how each keeps the scale in balance.

**Pedagogical purpose:** Make "the equation always balances" concrete.

**Style notes:** Diagrammatic. Could use a simple seesaw or two-pan balance.

## Figure 3.2 (optional): Debit / credit rules table

**Placement:** §3.4.

**Description:** Replicate the table from §3.4 — account types in rows; "Increases on," "Decreases on," "Normal balance" in columns. Include the DEAD CRLS mnemonic in a footnote.

**Pedagogical purpose:** Reference table students return to throughout the chapter and into Chapter 4.

**Style notes:** Clean tabular. Color-code debit-normal vs. credit-normal accounts.

## Figure 3.3 (optional): The accounting cycle (first four steps)

**Placement:** §3.5.

**Description:** A horizontal four-step flow: Identify & analyze → Journalize → Post to ledger → Trial balance. Each step labeled with its document/output.

**Pedagogical purpose:** Locate where this chapter sits within the larger cycle (Chapters 4 and 5 cover the remaining steps).

**Style notes:** Numbered flow chart. Could extend with grayed-out steps 5-9 to preview Chapters 4-5.

## Figure 3.4 (optional): Supreme Cleaners trial balance

**Placement:** §3.6, end.

**Description:** The trial balance from §3.6 reproduced as a clean, formatted table — the way it would appear in a real working paper.

**Pedagogical purpose:** Show students what a real trial balance looks like.

**Style notes:** Clean tabular, with the totals row in bold or shaded.
