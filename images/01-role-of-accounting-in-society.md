# Figure briefs — Chapter 1: The Role of Accounting in Society

The chapter prose does not contain explicit `[FIGURE: ...]` placeholders, but the source material referenced six figures (OSX_Acct_F01_00 through OSX_Acct_F01_05). The rewrite deferred those figures so the chapter reads cleanly as prose. Below are figure briefs for visual material that would *strengthen* the chapter if the production stage adds illustrations. Each is optional and pegged to a specific section.

## Figure 1.1 (optional): The accounting cycle in three steps

**Placement:** §1.2, after the second paragraph (after the record-summarize-communicate description).

**Description:** A simple horizontal three-panel diagram. Panel 1: "Record" — small icons of a receipt, an invoice, a cash payment, all flowing into a journal/ledger. Panel 2: "Summarize" — the journal/ledger feeding into the four core financial statements (Income Statement, Statement of Owner's Equity, Balance Sheet, Statement of Cash Flows). Panel 3: "Communicate" — the four statements flowing outward to four reader-icons (an investor, a banker, a regulator, an internal manager).

**Pedagogical purpose:** Lock in the three-step structure of the accounting cycle visually before students hit Chapter 2's full statement formats. Names the audience explicitly.

**Style notes:** Diagrammatic, not photorealistic. Consistent icon style. Each panel labeled. Arrows showing direction of flow.

## Figure 1.2 (optional): Financial vs. managerial accounting comparison table

**Placement:** §1.3, after the worked example with the car manufacturer.

**Description:** A two-column comparison table — left column "Financial Accounting," right column "Managerial Accounting" — comparing them on six rows: *Audience* (external / internal); *Standards* (GAAP / none required); *Frequency* (periodic, typically quarterly and annual / as-needed); *Format* (standardized / custom); *Information* (financial only / financial + non-financial); *Time orientation* (historical / historical + forward-looking).

**Pedagogical purpose:** A reference table students return to when the distinction starts to blur (which it does, in real organizations). Earns its place by being genuinely useful.

**Style notes:** Clean, tabular. Color-code or shade-differentiate the two columns. No decoration. Could include a small visual cue at the top of each column (a public stakeholder icon vs an internal-meeting icon).

## Figure 1.3 (optional): Stakeholders and the regulatory architecture

**Placement:** §1.4, after the stakeholder list and before the regulatory-architecture description.

**Description:** A concentric-ring or layered-pyramid diagram. Center: the organization. Inner ring: internal stakeholders (managers, employees). Middle ring: direct external stakeholders (stockholders, creditors, lenders, customers, suppliers). Outer ring: governmental and regulatory stakeholders (SEC, IRS, FASB, PCAOB, GASB, AICPA). Arrows from each ring inward represent "reads financial information"; arrows outward represent "produces financial information."

**Pedagogical purpose:** Makes the reach of accounting visible — every party in the diagram is downstream of the same record-summarize-communicate machinery.

**Style notes:** Diagrammatic. Could be a flat layered diagram or a concentric-ring diagram, depending on production house preference. Each entity labeled with a one-line role. The five regulators (SEC, FASB, PCAOB, GASB, AICPA) get small acronym callouts.

## Figure 1.4 (optional): Career paths in accounting

**Placement:** §1.5, after the eight career-path enumeration.

**Description:** A tree or fan diagram with "Accounting Education" at the root, branching into the eight career paths described in the chapter (Auditing, Taxation, Financial Accounting, Consulting, Accounting Information Systems, Cost and Managerial Accounting, Financial Planning, Entrepreneurship). Below each path, the relevant certification(s): CPA under Auditing/Taxation/Financial Accounting; CMA under Cost and Managerial; CIA under Auditing; CFE under fraud-related work within Auditing or Consulting; CFA and CFP under Financial Planning. Big Four firms shown as employers under Auditing/Taxation/Consulting paths.

**Pedagogical purpose:** Career-orienting visual — students can locate themselves and see the range of options.

**Style notes:** Tree diagram, vertical or horizontal. Could use simple icons for each branch. Certifications shown as smaller text under each branch. Avoid decorative styling; this is a reference figure.

## Style notes (chapter-wide)

- The chapter itself is text-only and reads cleanly without figures. Figures should be optional enhancements, not required reading.
- Avoid stock photography of "accountants at desks" — the chapter explicitly rejects the bean-counter cliché in §1.5; visual material should reflect the actual range of the work.
- If illustrating Capone or Madoff, use historically accurate visual material. Capone's mugshot from 1931 is in the public domain; Madoff's 2009 court appearance images are widely licensed. Either is appropriate as a small inset image to the §1.1 cold open if production wants visual anchoring.
