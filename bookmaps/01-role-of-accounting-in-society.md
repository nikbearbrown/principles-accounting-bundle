## Source-level notes

- **No author byline** in the source. The first-person-as-Bear rule did not trigger; the rewrite is in standard third-person AxF voice.
- **Source vintage ~2018** based on internal references (Jan 2018 Netflix stock-price chart, "as of 2012" FBI agent statistic in Capone sidebar, 2017 Top 100 Firms citation). The rewrite preserved all dated references where they were source-faithful and did not attempt to update statistics that depended on a specific year.
- **No internal contradictions** between the six source files. The pieces fit together cleanly; the rewrite organized them differently but did not need to resolve any disagreements.
- **Capone (1931) and Madoff (2009) are both real, well-documented cases.** Both are referenced in the source files (Capone in `06-m67836.md`, Madoff in `05-m67835.md`). The rewrite combined them in the cold open because together they make a stronger point about the chapter's central claim than either does alone — Capone establishes that accounting is the bookkeeping the law can read; Madoff establishes that the regulatory architecture exists because the bookkeeping is not always trustworthy on its own.
- **Sarbanes-Oxley context** (Enron, WorldCom, Tyco, the 2002 act) was introduced in the rewrite from the source's brief mention of SOX in `02-m67832.md`. The rewrite explained more directly than the source what specific gap PCAOB was created to fill, but did so using only what is publicly known and matched the framing of the source's claim.
- **All quantitative claims** in the chapter (Capone's $100M syndicate income; the eleven-year sentence; 1927 Supreme Court ruling on illegal earnings; Madoff's $50B and 2009 conviction; the FASB's 2002 IFRS convergence work; "more than 120 countries" using IFRS; Big Four firm list) trace to source files. No fabricated statistics.
