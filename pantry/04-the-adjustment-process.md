# Pantry — Chapter 4: The Adjustment Process

## Scenes

- **Mark Summers's accountant looking at the supplies closet — $800 on the trial balance, $300 in the storage room** — used in §4.1 cold open. Reusable as the canonical "trial balance is mechanically right but substantively wrong" scene.

## Analogies

- **Adjusting entries as "the books catching up to reality"** — §4.2. Limit: works for accruals, less natural for deferrals where reality has been ahead of cash all along.
- **Cash event vs economic event decoupling** — the structural reason adjusting entries exist. §4.2.

## Etymologies

- **Accrue** — from Latin *accrescere* (to grow toward). Accrued expenses and revenues are obligations or earnings that have grown toward becoming cash but haven't completed the journey.
- **Defer** — from Latin *differre* (to put off). Deferrals are recognitions put off until the earning or consumption catches up.

## Trade-offs named

- **Adjusting entries buy accrual accuracy at the cost of judgment and estimation.** §4.5.
- **Calendar-year fiscal year buys tax-year alignment at the cost of mid-cycle inventory counts; natural-business-cycle fiscal year buys clean inventory and unrushed close at the cost of comparability with calendar peers.** §4.3.

## Worked examples

- **$1,200 12-month insurance policy → $100/month adjustment**
- **$800 supplies → $300 remaining → $500 supplies expense adjustment**
- **$12,000 commercial press, 10-year life → $100/month depreciation; Accumulated Depreciation as contra-asset**
- **Wages owed Oct 26-31, $250 accrued at Oct 31, paid in Nov 5 payroll**
- **$30 of October interest earned, credited Nov 1**
- **$4,000 printing prepayment, $1,000 earned in first month** (from source)
