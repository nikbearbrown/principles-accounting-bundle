# Pantry — Chapter 5: Completing the Accounting Cycle

## Scenes

- **Mark Summers's books at end of October, start of November** — used in §5.1 and §5.8 — anchored to which balances carry over (permanent) vs which reset (temporary).

## Analogies

- **Temporary accounts as "scoreboards that reset between games"** vs permanent accounts as "running totals that follow the team through every season."
- **Income Summary as a "workspace"** that exists only during closing — never appears on a balance sheet, never appears on an income statement.

## Etymologies

- **Permanent / Real account** — *real* from Latin *res* (thing); a real account refers to an actual position (asset, liability, equity) rather than a flow during a period.
- **Temporary / Nominal account** — *nominal* from Latin *nomen* (name); a nominal account names an activity rather than a position.

## Trade-offs named

- **Closing entries buy fresh temporary accounts at the cost of an extra layer of bookkeeping work each period-end.** §5.3.
- **Classified balance sheet buys fast readability at the cost of requiring judgment about classification at the current/noncurrent line.** §5.5.

## Worked examples

- **Supreme Cleaners full closing entries** — Service Revenue $3,100, Interest Revenue $30, expenses totaling $3,650, Owner's Withdrawals $500, net loss $520. §5.3.
- **Current ratio for Supreme Cleaners** — $24,330 / $1,050 = 23.2 (anomalously high because the business just launched). §5.5.
- **Single-transaction walk through the nine-step cycle** — November 3 customer pays $40 cash. §5.8.

## Reusable terms

Temporary account; permanent account; nominal account; real account; closing entries; Income Summary; post-closing trial balance; classified balance sheet; current assets; current liabilities; current ratio; PP&E; book value (net carrying value); the nine-step accounting cycle.
