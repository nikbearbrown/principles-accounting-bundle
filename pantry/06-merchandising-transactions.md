# Pantry — Chapter 6: Merchandising Transactions

## Scenes

- **Jason and James of J&J Games buying from Marcus Electronics** — running example. Reusable for any chapter on merchandising or supply-chain accounting.

## Analogies

- **Gross profit as "the margin between cost and price, before anything else happens to it"** — the analytical centerpiece of merchandising income statements.
- **2/10, n/30 as a "bounty for early payment"** — encoding the seller's preference for cash-now over cash-later.

## Trade-offs named

- **Perpetual buys real-time visibility at the cost of continuous tracking; periodic buys operational simplicity at the cost of no period-internal inventory or COGS figure.** §6.3.
- **Multi-step income statement buys analytical visibility at the cost of visual complexity.** §6.6.
- **Skipping a 2/10 discount costs roughly 36% annualized.** §6.9.

## Worked examples

- **$3,000 purchase with 2/10 terms, $60 discount taken**
- **$200 console (cost $120) sold cash, sold credit, returned, allowance**
- **J&J multi-step income statement: $462,000 net sales, $182,000 gross profit, $103,200 net income**

Inventory; Cost of Goods Sold; Gross Profit; Net Sales; Sales Returns and Allowances; Sales Discounts; Purchase Discounts; Freight In; Freight Out; perpetual inventory system; periodic inventory system; FOB shipping point; FOB destination; multi-step income statement; contra-revenue accounts; gross profit margin; operating expense ratio.
