## Analogies

- **The accounting equation as "everything the business has came from one of two places — borrowed or owned"** — sources-and-claims framing of A = L + E.
- **The trial balance as a "checkpoint"** — catches mechanical errors but not content errors.
- **DEAD CRLS mnemonic** — Debits Expenses Assets Dividends; Credits Revenues Liabilities Stock(holders' equity). Memorable shorthand for normal balances.

## Etymologies

- **Debit** — from Latin *debere* (to owe). Originally referred to amounts owed *to* the firm.
- **Credit** — from Latin *credere* (to trust, to believe). Originally referred to amounts the firm *trusted* customers or vendors with.
- **Journal** — from Latin *diurnalis* (daily). The general journal is the daily record of transactions.
- **Ledger** — from Old English / Dutch roots meaning "to lie down" or "rest in place" — the master book where account balances live.

## Trade-offs named

- **Historical cost buys verifiability at the cost of being out of date.** §3.2.
- **Conservatism prevents overstated income at the cost of potentially delayed recognition of genuine gains.** §3.2.
- **Double-entry bookkeeping buys traceable accuracy at the cost of recording every transaction twice.** §3.4.
- **The four-step accounting cycle buys traceability at the cost of deferred summarization.** §3.5.
